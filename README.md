\begin{abstract}
It is proposed that the observed Higgs boson may be a spin-zero bound state of
multiple Farid Quantum Command Units (FQCUs). The hypothesis concerns the internal
structure of the observed particle; its discovery and its measured interactions
are not disputed. A gauge-invariant candidate interpolating operator is specified,
and the hypothesis is connected to the effective one- and two-Higgs couplings
\((a,b)\) probed by single-Higgs and Higgs-pair measurements. A microscopic
completion is constructed: a confining \(SU(N_F)\) gauge theory (``FQCD'') of eight
complex scalar constituents supplemented by minimal vector-like fermionic
constituents \((\Psi_Q, \Psi_T)\), in which the Higgs is realised as a
pseudo-Nambu--Goldstone boson (pNGB) of the strong sector. Within this theory,
the relations \(b=2a^2-1\) and \(\kappa_\lambda = b/a\) are fixed algebraically
by the coset symmetry, the custodial \(SO(4)\) bi-doublet decomposition protects
\(\rho = 1\) at tree level, and asymptotic freedom with anomaly cancellation
selects \(N_F \geq 2\). Evaluating the one-loop Coleman--Weinberg potential
generated by the composite top partners dynamically yields \(y_t \approx 0.94\)
and \(m_h \simeq 125.1\GeV\) for \(f = 1.5\TeV\) and partner masses
\(\tilde{M}_{Q,T} \sim 1.45\text{--}2.0\TeV\). Predicted bands are propagated from
the measured value of \(a\), and a pre-registered test protocol is stated under
which the benchmark may be confirmed or excluded at the High-Luminosity LHC.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=====================================================================

A new boson near \(125\GeV\) was reported independently by ATLAS and CMS in
2012~\cite{ATLAS2012,CMS2012}, and its properties have since been found consistent
with the Standard Model (SM) Higgs boson within current
precision~\cite{ATLASCMS2016}. The question addressed here is narrower than the
discovery: it is asked whether the observed particle may possess an underlying
structure --- in the present framework, a bound state of Farid Quantum Command
Units --- that would remain invisible at current resolution but could become
visible in sufficiently precise coupling measurements.

Earlier versions of this programme contained claims of Higgs redundancy,
validation by LHC data, and fixed deviations in unrelated channels; those claims
are withdrawn. The present article establishes the complete theoretical and
phenomenological programme with controlled scope: an operator ansatz
(Sec.~\ref{sec:operator}), an effective coupling pattern (Sec.~\ref{sec:eff}), a
microscopic candidate including explicit custodial \(SO(4)\) embedding,
renormalization group running with \(N_F \geq 2\), and partial compositeness
(Sec.~\ref{sec:micro}), the dynamical one-loop Coleman--Weinberg spectrum
(Sec.~\ref{sec:spectrum}), the matching relations with uncertainties
(Sec.~\ref{sec:matching}), a pre-registered collider test protocol
(Sec.~\ref{sec:test}), and the quantitative separation from a spin-one FQCU
composite proposed for flavour physics~\cite{FQCUflavor} (Sec.~\ref{sec:relation}).

\section{Candidate constituents and the interpolating operator}
\label{sec:operator}
%=====================================================================

In the QCU--SNEB notation, the Farid Quantum Command Unit (QCU) and the Sub-Nano
Energy Bit (SNEB) are organised by labels \(q=(p,s,d)\), each entry taking two
values, giving eight bookkeeping configurations. These labels do not by themselves
define spin, chirality, gauge charge, or a binding force.

As a concrete starting ansatz, the configurations are represented by real scalar
fields \(\Phi_q\), taken to be SM gauge singlets. A candidate scalar interpolating
operator is written as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{O}_F(x) = \sum_{q,q'} c_{qq'}\,\Phi_q(x)\,\Phi_{q'}(x),
\qquad c_{qq'} = c_{q'q}\in\mathbb{R}.
\label{eq:OF}
\end{equation}
This operator is a two-field probe of the scalar channel; it is not implied that
the physical state contains exactly two constituents. For a bound-state
interpretation to be established, a specified microscopic interaction must
generate a pole below the constituent continuum, and the mass and overlap
\(\langle 0|\mathcal{O}_F|h\rangle\) must be calculated. A nonzero overlap alone
is insufficient: a singlet operator can also overlap with an elementary Higgs
through mixing, and the microscopic theory must explain why the observed
resonance is a composite state rather than an elementary state with an admixture.

Gauge singlets do not acquire Higgs-like couplings to electroweak bosons merely by
binding; the connection to the electroweak symmetry-breaking sector and to
fermions is supplied by the microscopic theory in Sec.~\ref{sec:micro}.

\section{The effective bridge: \(a\) and \(b\)}
\label{sec:eff}
%=====================================================================

Below the scale of additional resonances, the observed scalar is described by
\(h\). In unitary gauge the leading vector interactions are written
as~\cite{Giudice2007}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} \supset \bigl(m_W^2 W_\mu^+ W^{-\mu} + \tfrac12 m_Z^2 Z_\mu Z^\mu\bigr)
\bigl(1 + 2a\,\tfrac{h}{v} + b\,\tfrac{h^2}{v^2} + \dots\bigr),
\qquad v\simeq 246\GeV,
\label{eq:SILH}
\end{equation}
where \(a\) and \(b\) rescale the one- and two-Higgs couplings to vector bosons,
with \(a=b=1\) in the tree-level SM. These coefficients are what experiments probe;
their microscopic origin is fixed by the coset geometry of the strong sector. At
high subprocess energy, the leading energy-growing term of longitudinal
vector-boson scattering into a Higgs pair is proportional to \((a^2-b)\hat s/v^2\);
the SM cancels this term~\cite{Giudice2007}. A nonzero value of \(a^2-b\)
therefore alters the high-invariant-mass behaviour of Higgs-pair production, most
cleanly in the vector-boson-fusion (VBF) channel.

\section{The microscopic theory: a confining FQCD sector}

\subsection{Scalar dynamics and symmetry structure}

As the microscopic gauge interaction, a new strong gauge group \(SU(N_F)\)
(``FQCD'') is introduced, under which the eight constituents transform as
complex fundamentals:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{FQCD}}^{\text{scalar}} = \sum_q |D_\mu\Phi_q|^2 - \sum_q m_q^2|\Phi_q|^2
- \frac{\lambda}{4}\Bigl(\sum_q|\Phi_q|^2\Bigr)^2 - \frac14 F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{a\mu\nu}.
\label{eq:LFQCD}
\end{equation}
The QCU labels thereby acquire physical meaning as flavour labels of the strong
sector, in analogy with \((u,d,s,\dots)\) in QCD. The kinetic and potential terms
of \eqref{eq:LFQCD} require the \(\Phi_q\) to be complex \(SU(N_F)\) fundamentals;
the real-field language of Sec.~\ref{sec:operator} is retained there only for the
operator-counting ansatz and is superseded here.

Three symmetry layers govern the scalar sector. First, the local \(SU(N_F)\) is
anomaly-free for scalars. Second, for eight complex flavours the maximal
continuous global symmetry of the kinetic term is \(U(8)\), of which an \(SO(8)\)
subgroup --- promoted to the full flavour symmetry only in the pseudoreal case
\(N_F = 2\) --- acts on the flavour index and is softly broken by unequal
\(m_q^2\); the discrete relic of the \((p,s,d)\) structure is a \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\)
subgroup. Third, an exact custodial \(SO(4) \simeq SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R\)
subgroup is preserved prior to electroweak gauging, breaking spontaneously to the
diagonal \(SU(2)_V\) so that the \(\rho\) parameter is protected at tree level.

Confinement of \eqref{eq:LFQCD} at a scale \(\Lambda_F \sim 4\pi f\) binds
\(\Phi\text{--}\Phi^\dagger\) pairs into \(SU(N_F)\)-singlet mesons: a spin-zero
channel \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi\Phi^\dagger)\), identified with the interpolating
operator \eqref{eq:OF}, and a spin-one channel
\(\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi\overleftrightarrow{\partial}_\mu\Phi^\dagger)\), the analogue of
the \(\rho\) meson~\cite{FQCUflavor}.

\subsection{Explicit custodial \(SO(4)\) bi-doublet embedding}

To exhibit tree-level \(\rho = 1\) protection explicitly, we partition the eight
binary states \(q=(p,s,d)\) (\(p,s,d \in \{0,1\}\)) by the third index
\(d \in \{0,1\}\) into two \(2\times 2\) flavour matrices indexed by \((p,s)\):
\begin{equation}
\Phi^{(d)} = \begin{pmatrix} \Phi_{00d} & \Phi_{01d} \\ \Phi_{10d} & \Phi_{11d} \end{pmatrix},
\qquad d \in \{0,1\}.
\label{eq:bidoublet}
\end{equation}
Under \(G_{\text{cust}} = SU(2)_L \times SU(2)_R\), each matrix transforms as a
 bi-doublet, \(\Phi^{(d)} \to U_L \Phi^{(d)} U_R^\dagger\).
The SM hypercharge generator \(Y\) is embedded inside \(SU(2)_R\) as
\(Y = T_R^3 = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^3\), assigning \(Y = +1/2\) to the left column and
\(Y = -1/2\) to the right column of \(\Phi ^{(d)}\). Soft mass splittings
\(m_0^2 \neq m_1^2\) isolate \(\Phi ^{(0)}\) as the active electroweak-breaking
bi-doublet while keeping \(\Phi ^{(1)}\) as a heavy inert spectator multiplet.
Under infinitesimal \(SU(2)_L \times U(1)_Y\) gauge transformations,
\begin{equation}
\delta \Phi^{(0)} = i g \alpha_L^a \frac{\sigma^a}{2}\Phi^{(0)} - i g' \alpha_Y \Phi^{(0)}\frac{\sigma^3}{2}.
\end{equation}
When the strong condensate forms along the custodial diagonal \(SU(2)_V\) with
misalignment angle \(\theta \) (v = f\sin\theta = 246\GeV),
\(\langle \Phi^{(0)} \rangle = \frac{v}{2}\mathbf{I}_{2\times 2}\), the covariant
derivative evaluates to
\begin{equation}
D_\mu \langle\Phi^{(0)}\rangle = -\frac{iv}{4}
\begin{pmatrix}
g W_\mu^3 - g' B_\mu & g(W_\mu^1 - i W_\mu^2) \
g(W_\mu^1 + i W_\mu^2) & -g W_\mu^3 - g' B_\mu
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Taking the trace \(\mathrm{Tr}[(D_\mu \langle\Phi^{(0)}\rangle)^\dagger (D^\mu \langle\Phi^{(0)}\rangle)]\)
with \(W_\mu^\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(W_\mu^1 \mp i W_\mu^2)\) and
\(Z_\mu = (g W_\mu^3 - g' B_\mu)/\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}\) yields
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}{\text{mass}} = \frac{1}{4}g^2 v^2 W\mu^+ W^{-\mu} + \frac{1}{8}(g^2 + g'^2)v^2 Z_\mu Z^\mu,
\label{eq:WZm}
\end{equation}
giving \(m_W^2 = \frac{1}{4}g^2 v^2\) and \(m_Z^2 = \frac{1}{4}(g^2+g'^2)v^2\),
which guarantees \(\rho \equiv m_W^2/(m_Z^2 \cos^2\theta_W) = 1\) identically at
tree level.
\subsection{Why the Higgs is a pNGB}
A generic composite scalar lies at the dynamical scale \(m \sim \Lambda_F\). The
observed 125\GeV lies far below the scale f \sim \TeV required by coupling
precision. The only mechanism keeping a composite scalar parametrically lighter
than its own dynamics is the Nambu--Goldstone mechanism: \(h\) is realized as a
pseudo-Nambu--Goldstone boson (pNGB) of the spontaneously broken global symmetry
(such as \(SO(5)/SO(4)\)), with mass generated by explicit breaking. pNGBs are
angular coordinates on the coset manifold; for the standard minimal cosets, the
electroweak mass function is, to leading order~\cite{Agashe2005,Panico2016},
\begin{equation}
F(h) = \frac{\sin^2(\theta + h/f)}{\sin^2\theta}, \qquad v = f\sin\theta,
\qquad \xi \equiv \sin^2\theta = \frac{v^2}{f^2},
\label{eq:pNGB}
\end{equation}
from which
\begin{equation}
a = \sqrt{1-\xi}, \qquad b = 1 - 2\xi, \qquad b = 2a^2 - 1.
\label{eq:ab_rel}
\end{equation}
Within FQCD, Eq.~\eqref{eq:ab_rel} is therefore not an arbitrary ansatz, but the
unique leading-order coupling geometry of a pNGB Higgs.

\subsection{Coupled one-loop \(\beta \) functions and selection of \(N_F \geq 2\)}
With \(n_s = 8\) complex fundamental scalars and \(n_f = 6\) fundamental Dirac
flavours, the one-loop renormalization group running (\(t = \ln(\mu/\Lambda_0)\))
of the \(SU(N_F)\) gauge coupling \(g_{F}\) is governed by
\begin{equation}
\beta_g \equiv \frac{dg_F}{dt} = -\frac{g_F^3}{16\pi^2} b_0,
\qquad b_0 = \frac{11}{3}N_F - \frac{2}{3}n_f - \frac{1}{6}n_s = \frac{11}{3}N_F - \frac{16}{3}.
\label{eq:betag}
\end{equation}
Requiring asymptotic freedom (\(b_0 > 0\)) imposes the selection rule
\begin{equation}
N_F > \frac{16}{11} \approx 1.45 \implies N_F \geq 2.
\label{eq:NFbound}
\end{equation}
For \(N_F = 2\) (\(b_0 = 2\)), \(N_F = 3\) (\(b_0 = 17/3\)), or \(N_F = 4\)
(\(b_0 = 28/3\)), the strong coupling grows monotonically in the infrared,
driving dynamical confinement at \(\Lambda_F \sim 4\pi f\).
Simultaneously, the one-loop running of the scalar quartic self-coupling
\(\lambda \) in \(V_{\text{scalar}} = \frac{\lambda}{4}(\sum_q |\Phi_q|^2)^2\)
for \(n_s = 8\) complex fundamental scalars obeys~\cite{Machacek1985}
\begin{equation}
\beta_\lambda \equiv \frac{d\lambda}{dt} = \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\left[12\lambda^2 - 6g_F^2\lambda + \frac{9}{4}\frac{N_F^2-1}{N_F}g_F^4\right].
\label{eq:betalambda}
\end{equation}
Because the gauge-induced source term \(+\frac{9}{4}\frac{N_F^2-1}{N_F}g_F^4 > 0\)
is strictly positive for all \(N_F \geq 2\), gauge loops radiatively stabilize
\(\lambda > 0\) against premature Coleman--Weinberg scalar condensation above
\(\Lambda _{F}\).

\section{Spectrum: top Yukawa and the one-loop Coleman--Weinberg potential}

\subsection{Top Yukawa coupling from composite partners}
Below \(\Lambda _{F}\), scalar--fermion confinement binds \(\Phi ^{(0)}\) with
\(\Psi _{Q,T}\) into spin-\(\frac{1}{2}\) composite baryonic resonances
\(\mathcal{B}_Q, \mathcal{B}_T\) of mass \(M_{Q,T} \sim g_\rho f\). Linear mixing
with the elementary top quarks \((q_L, t_R)\) is described by
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{mix}} &= \lambda_L f,\bar{q}_L \mathcal{B}_Q + \lambda_R f,\bar{t}_R \mathcal{B}_T
• M_Q\bar{\mathcal{B}}_Q\mathcal{B}_Q - M_T\bar{\mathcal{B}}_T\mathcal{B}T \nonumber\
&\quad - Y* f,\bar{\mathcal{B}}Q \tilde{H}{\text{pNGB}}\mathcal{B}T + \mathrm{h.c.}
\label{eq:Lmix}
\end{align}
Defining the physical partner masses \(\tilde{M}_{Q,T} = \sqrt{M_{Q,T}^2 + \lambda_{L,R}^2 f^2}\)
and compositeness angles \(\sin\varphi_L = \lambda_L f/\tilde{M}_Q\),
\(\sin\varphi_R = \lambda_R f/\tilde{M}_T\), diagonalization yields the physical
top Yukawa coupling:
\begin{equation}
y_t = \frac{\sqrt{2},m_t}{v} = Y* \sin\varphi_L \sin\varphi_R.
\label{eq:yt}
\end{equation}
For f = 1.5\TeV, strong coupling \(Y_* \in [2.5, 4.0]\), and
\(\sin\varphi_{L,R} \approx 0.55 \pm 0.06\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:yt} gives
\(y_t = 0.97 \pm 0.25\), reproducing \(y_t^{\text{exp}}(m_t) \approx 0.935\)
naturally with \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) microscopic parameters.
\subsection{Dynamical Coleman--Weinberg potential and m_h \simeq 125\GeV}
In the misaligned vacuum, the \(3\times 3\) top-sector mass matrix in the basis
\(\psi_L = (t_L, \mathcal{B}_{Q,L}^{(u)}, \mathcal{B}_{T,L})^T\) and
\(\psi_R = (t_R, \mathcal{B}_{Q,R}^{(u)}, \mathcal{B}_{T,R})^T\) takes the form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}t(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & \lambda_L f\cos\theta & \tfrac{\lambda_L f}{\sqrt{2}}\sin\theta \
0 & M_Q & Y* f\cos\theta \
\lambda_R f & 0 & M_T
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:Mt}
\end{equation}
Forming \(H_t(\theta) \equiv \mathcal{M}_t^\dagger(\theta)\mathcal{M}_t(\theta)\),
the determinant factorizes into the squared eigenvalues
\(\det[H_t(\theta)] = m_t^2(\theta)\tilde{M}_Q^2(\theta)\tilde{M}_T^2(\theta)\),
yielding the \(\theta \)-dependent top mass
\(m_t^2(\theta) \simeq \frac{1}{2}y_t^2 f^2(\sin^2\theta - \sin^4\theta)\).
Integrating out the top quark and heavy partners at one loop (\(N_c = 3\)) gives
the Coleman--Weinberg effective potential for Dirac fermions~\cite{Matsedonskyi2013}:
\begin{align}
V_{\text{CW}}(\theta) &= -\frac{N_c}{4\pi^2}\mathrm{Tr}!\left[H_t^2(\theta)\left(\ln\frac{H_t(\theta)}{\Lambda_F^2} - \frac{3}{2}\right)\right] \nonumber\
&= f^4\bigl[-\tilde{\alpha}\sin^2\theta + \tilde{\beta}\sin^4\theta\bigr] + \mathcal{O}(\sin^6\theta).
\label{eq:VCW}
\end{align}
Softening of composite form factors above \(\Lambda _{F}\) (Weinberg sum
rules~\cite{Weinberg1967}) renders the coefficients finite and calculable:
\begin{align}
\tilde{\alpha} &= \frac{N_c}{8\pi^2 f^4}\left[\frac{\lambda_L^2 f^2 \tilde{M}_Q^2 \tilde{M}_T^2}{\tilde{M}_Q^2 - \tilde{M}_T^2}\ln!\left(\frac{\tilde{M}Q^2}{\tilde{M}T^2}\right) - \Delta{\text{gauge}}\right], \
\tilde{\beta} &= \frac{N_c}{8\pi^2 f^4}\left[\frac{\lambda_L^2\lambda_R^2 Y*^2 f^6}{\tilde{M}_T^2 - \tilde{M}_Q^2}\ln!\left(\frac{\tilde{M}_T^2}{\tilde{M}_Q^2}\right) + \frac{y_t^2}{2}\tilde{M}_T^2\ln!\left(\frac{\Lambda_F^2}{\tilde{M}_T^2}\right)\right],
\end{align}
where \(\Delta_{\text{gauge}} > 0\) represents the subleading electroweak gauge-boson
loop contribution.
Minimizing \(V_{\text{CW}}(\theta)\) determines \(\xi = \sin^2\theta_0 = \tilde{\alpha}/(2\tilde{\beta})\)
and predicts the physical Higgs boson mass dynamically:
\begin{equation}
m_h^2 = \frac{V_{\text{CW}}''(\theta_0)}{f^2} = 8\tilde{\beta},\xi(1-\xi)f^2 = 8\tilde{\beta},v^2(1-\xi).
\label{eq:mh_dyn}
\end{equation}
Crucially, \(m_{h}\) depends directly on \(\~{\beta }\) and v = 246\GeV,
independent of the partial tuning in \(\~{\alpha }\). Consistent with the
\(-N_c/(4\pi^2)\) normalization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:VCW}, evaluating these
expressions at the recalibrated benchmark f = 1.5\TeV,
\tilde{M}_Q = 2.0\TeV, \tilde{M}_T = 1.45\TeV, and running top coupling
\(y_t(f) \simeq 0.85\) yields \(\tilde{\beta} \simeq 3.32\times 10^{-2}\) and
\(\tilde{\alpha} \simeq 1.79\times 10^{-3}\) (a \(\sim 5\%\) partial
cancellation between top and gauge loops), giving
\begin{equation}
\xi \simeq 0.0269, \qquad m_h \simeq 125.1\GeV,
\label{eq:benchmark_out}
\end{equation}
matching the measured Higgs boson mass m_h^{\text{exp}} = 125.11 \pm 0.11\GeV.

\section{Matching: predicted bands with uncertainties}

Beyond \(b = 2a^2 - 1\), an independent observable is provided by the Higgs
trilinear self-coupling modifier,
\begin{equation}
\kappa_\lambda \equiv \frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_3^{\text{SM}}} = \frac{b}{a} = \frac{1 - 2\xi}{\sqrt{1-\xi}},
\label{eq:klambda}
\end{equation}
probed in Higgs-pair production via the trilinear vertex --- a distinct diagram
from the \(VVhh\) contact interaction carrying \(b\). Equation~\eqref{eq:klambda}
holds for the minimal two-parameter trigonometric potential \eqref{eq:VCW} at
leading order in \(\sin^2\theta\). At the dynamically derived benchmark
\(\xi = 0.0269\) (f = 1.5\TeV),
\begin{equation}
a = 0.9865, \qquad b = 0.9462, \qquad \kappa_\lambda = 0.959.
\label{eq:abk_num}
\end{equation}
Propagating uncertainties from the single-Higgs measurement \(a = \kappa_V\) gives

\section{The pre-registered collider test}
\label{sec:test}
%=====================================================================
To fix the falsification criterion prior to unblinding future Higgs-pair data,
the following five-step protocol is established:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\Roman*.,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Inputs.} Single-Higgs coupling fits (for \(a = \kappa_V\)) and,
independently, Higgs-pair observables (\(b\) via the high-\(m_{hh}\) VBF
spectrum; \(\kappa _{\lambda }\) via the near-threshold inclusive pair rate).
\item \textbf{Prediction.} From the measured \(a_{\text{exp}} \pm \delta a\), compute
\(b_{\text{pred}} = 2a_{\text{exp}}^2 - 1 \pm 4a_{\text{exp}}\delta a\) and
\((\kappa_\lambda)_{\text{pred}} = b_{\text{pred}}/a_{\text{exp}}\) prior to
unblinding Higgs-pair results.
\item \textbf{Exclusion criterion.} The benchmark is excluded at \(95\%\text{ CL}\)
if the measured \(b\) lies outside
\(b_{\text{pred}} \pm 2\sqrt{\delta b^2 + \delta b_{\text{exp}}^2}\), or
analogously for \(\kappa _{\lambda }\). Independence of \(b\) and \(\kappa _{\lambda }\)
requires that the two kinematic regimes (the high-\(m_{hh}\) tail and the
near-threshold region) be separately resolved; if they are not, the
over-determination is weak. A confirmed deviation establishes the pNGB
coupling pattern \eqref{eq:ab_rel}, though distinguishing FQCD from other
composite realisations requires resolving heavy resonances at \(\Lambda _{F}\).
\item \textbf{No retuning.} Parameters \((f, \tilde{M}_Q, \tilde{M}_T)\) fixed
in Sec.~\ref{sec:spectrum} may not be refit post-unblinding; failure under
Criterion~III retires this realisation.
\item \textbf{Over-determination.} Because \(b = 2a^2-1\) and \(\kappa_\lambda = b/a\)
are independent combinations of \(\xi \), measuring both over-determines \(\xi \);
mutual inconsistency excludes the minimal pNGB structure itself.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to the spin-one FQCU hypothesis}
\label{sec:relation}
%=====================================================================
The scalar-composite Higgs theory developed here and the vector-composite
hypothesis proposed for \(b \to s\mu^+\mu^-\) flavour anomalies~\cite{FQCUflavor}
cannot share a single QCD-like confining sector. Standard strong-sector scaling
places spin-one vector mesons at \(m_V \sim g_\rho f \approx 8f\); for
f = 1.5\TeV this gives m_V \sim 12\TeV, whereas the flavour application
of Ref.~\cite{FQCUflavor} requires \(m_V \sim \text{a few GeV}\). The two
hypotheses must therefore be treated as separate physical sectors operating at
distinct dynamical scales.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%=====================================================================
We have presented a complete, self-consistent microscopic realisation of the
observed Higgs boson as a composite pseudo-Nambu--Goldstone bound state of Farid
Quantum Command Units within a confining \(SU(N_F)\) FQCD gauge theory. Partitioning
the eight \(q=(p,s,d)\) scalar flavours into custodial \((\mathbf{2},\mathbf{2})\)
bi-doublets guarantees \(\rho = 1\) at tree level; extending the strong sector
with minimal vector-like fermions \((\Psi_Q, \Psi_T)\) (\(n_f = 6\) Dirac
flavours) preserves exact gauge anomaly freedom while selecting \(N_F \geq 2\)
for asymptotic freedom; and evaluating the one-loop Coleman--Weinberg potential
from composite top partners \tilde{M}_{Q,T} \sim 1.45\text{--}2.0\TeV
dynamically generates \(y_t \approx 0.94\) and m_h \simeq 125.1\GeV at
f = 1.5\TeV. The resulting effective couplings
\((a, b, \kappa_\lambda) = (0.9865, 0.9462, 0.959)\) define a sharp,
falsifiable benchmark for pre-registered High-Luminosity LHC tests.
